About

A son of the Zongo, an economist by training, a servant by choice.

Hon. Yussif Issaka Jajah hails from Bole and was raised in the tightly-knit communities of Maamobi, Nima and Accra New Town — the very neighbourhoods he now represents. That upbringing shapes everything about how he governs: close to the ground, present at the funerals and the naming ceremonies, and unembarrassed to walk a gutter line in the rain to see a problem for himself.

He is a development finance and energy professional. He holds an MBA in International Oil and Gas Management from the University of Dundee in Scotland, an MSc in Energy Economics and an MPhil in Development Finance from GIMPA, and a BSc from the University of Professional Studies, Accra. Before politics he worked as an oil and gas consultant and as a research fellow with the Inter-Ministerial Coordinating Committee on Decentralisation.

In Parliament he sits on the Finance Committee and the Roads and Transport Committee, and is active on energy, defence and interior matters. In 2025 he was appointed Deputy Minister for Tourism, Culture and Creative Arts.

The Record

Work delivered, work under way

A constituency of narrow lanes and dense settlements needs practical answers — drains that carry water away, pitches for young people, and a voice in Accra that does not go quiet between elections.

Infrastructure

A drainage network to end decades of flooding

After a needs assessment showed drainage — not resurfacing — was the constituency's most critical gap, a planned ten-kilometre inner-roads contract was converted into a major inner-community drainage project covering Maamobi, Kawukudi, Accra New Town, the Kwatso electoral area and surrounding neighbourhoods. A local contractor was deliberately chosen so the community stays engaged in the works.

Sod cut 2025 · Delivered under the Government's road and drainage commitments

Youth & Sports

135 youth groups kitted out

In November 2024 the MP's office distributed sets of jerseys and footballs to 135 community youth groups across Ayawaso North — a constituency that has produced professionals including Majeed Ashimeru and Abdul Manaf Nurudeen. It followed an earlier round in May 2024 that equipped nine football clubs and added a GH¢2,000 cash prize.

November 2024 · With a commitment to build a proper training pitch

Parliament

Finance, roads and transport

An accountant and energy economist by training, he brings technical weight to the Finance Committee and the Roads and Transport Committee — the two committees where a constituency like Ayawaso North either gets budgeted for, or gets forgotten. He is also active on energy, defence and interior matters.

7th, 8th and 9th Parliaments of the Fourth Republic

National Service

Deputy Minister for Tourism, Culture & Creative Arts

Appointed to the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Creative Arts, he carries a brief with real economic upside for young Ghanaians — creative industries, cultural heritage and the tourism value chain — into a portfolio he approaches with a development-finance lens.

Appointed 2025

Advocacy

Naming the gaps out loud

He has publicly pressed the case that Kwatso and Maamobi East still have no government school, and that the constituency lacks a fire station, an ambulance post and a community centre. Putting those absences on the record is the first step to getting them funded.

Ongoing

Mandate

A mandate that keeps growing

Elected in 2016 with 22,144 votes (59.97%), returned in 2020 with 28,207 votes (62.66%), and re-elected again in 2024 for a third term. Few things speak louder about a representative than a constituency that keeps sending him back.

The Constituency

Ayawaso North

Ayawaso North sits in the heart of Greater Accra — Maamobi, Nima, Kotobabi, Accra New Town, Kawukudi, Kwatso and the surrounding communities. It is young, dense, enterprising and proudly Zongo. Its challenges are the challenges of a city that grew faster than its drains and its classrooms.

The work here is unglamorous and specific: clearing water off the streets, giving young people somewhere to play, and making sure the constituency's name is spoken in the rooms where budgets are decided.
